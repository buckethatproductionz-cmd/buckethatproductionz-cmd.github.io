Livestream highlights, cut and posted for you.

GTA Clipper finds the best moments in long gaming livestreams, turns them into short vertical clips with captions, and publishes them to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok.

Features

Moment detection

Transcribes the whole stream and scores laughter, shouting, chat activity and on-screen action to find clip-worthy moments.

Vertical editing

Reframes 16:9 stream footage to 9:16 so clips fill a phone screen.

Word-timed captions

Burns in readable captions timed to each spoken word, checked against the transcript.

Review before posting

Every clip is approved by a person before it is queued. Nothing is posted without that approval.

Scheduled publishing

Approved clips are posted on a schedule through each platform's official API, with a title and caption for each platform.

TikTok drafts

On TikTok, clips are sent to the creator's inbox as drafts, so the creator reviews and posts them in the TikTok app.

How it works

  1. Add a stream. Paste a link to a finished Twitch or YouTube livestream VOD.
  2. Analyse. GTA Clipper downloads the VOD, transcribes it and ranks candidate moments.
  3. Pick and approve. The best moments are cut, captioned and shown for review. Only approved clips continue.
  4. Connect accounts. The creator signs in with YouTube, Instagram and TikTok through each platform's official login and grants posting permission.
  5. Publish. Clips go out on a schedule. TikTok clips arrive as drafts in the creator's TikTok inbox to finish and post.

Platforms and permissions

TikTok

Uses Login Kit and the Content Posting API (user.info.basic, video.upload, video.publish) to send clips to the connected account.

YouTube

Uses the YouTube Data API to upload Shorts to the connected channel.

Instagram

Uses the Instagram Graph API to publish Reels to the connected professional account.

Access can be revoked at any time from each platform's connected-apps settings. See the Privacy Policy for exactly what data is used and how.

FAQ

Does GTA Clipper post without my approval?
No. Every clip is reviewed before it is queued, and TikTok clips arrive as drafts you post yourself.

What does it store?
Only the sign-in tokens each platform issues and the clips being published. It does not collect data about viewers. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

Who owns the content?
The person connecting an account is responsible for having the rights to everything they publish. See the Terms of Service.

How do I contact you?
Through github.com/buckethatproductionz-cmd.
